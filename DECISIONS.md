# v1 design decisions

The decisions behind the v1 build, and *why* — so future-you (and anyone else
building this) knows what was chosen on purpose vs. what's just incidental.

| # | Decision | Choice | Why |
|---|----------|--------|-----|
| 1 | **What v1 is** | A puck that **records** a session on one board; read the jumps on land afterward. Personal, one board. Open-source the files as you go. | Fastest path to the real unknown — *does the airtime method work on foil jumps?* Don't let "product for others" gate the first number. *(Update: simulation now answers this — wings are near-ballistic, so the airtime method holds; see #28. Real-water measurement is the remaining confirmation.)* |
| 2 | **How you read it** | Review afterward over USB. No live/on-water display. | You can't usefully read anything mid-wing anyway; Woo itself just syncs on land. |
| 3 | **Accuracy target** | Within ~10%, proven **once** against slow-mo video, then bake in a correction factor. | 10% is invisible in real life; the method's own physics (drag, wave landings) limits you there regardless. Accuracy comes from the video check, not an expensive sensor. |
| 4 | **Sensor** | MPU-6050 (bought 4). Only the accelerometer is used **(ESP32/MPU-6050 build only)**. | Cheapest, best-documented, clears the 10% target. Using only \|acceleration\| makes mounting orientation irrelevant. *(Accel-only holds for non-spinning jumps; the Sense build needs its gyro on the detector hot-path for spun jumps — see #29.)* |
| 5 | **The maths** | Airtime method: `height = g × airtime² ÷ 8`. Detect takeoff by free-fall (\|a\|≈0), landing by the spike. | Avoids double-integration drift, which is unusable on cheap IMUs. |
| 6 | **Board + power** | DFRobot **FireBeetle 2 ESP32-E** (DFR0654: USB-C, built-in LiPo charging, 4 MB WROOM-32E module) + a **2500 mAh** single-cell LiPo (785060, PCM-protected). Charge & download over USB when the capsule is open. | Already owned; built-in charging removes a whole class of wiring/safety mistakes; the FireBeetle 2 is one of the lowest-power ESP32 boards for the later sleep upgrade. *(Identified as the 2/E variant during bring-up — USB-C + CH340 + the 4 MB chip all match DFR0654, not the original DFR0478.)* |
| 7 | **On/off** | Motion-activated recording. Simple "record only while moving" now; real low-power deep-sleep later. | No hole needed in a sealed capsule. Simple version keeps logs clean; deep-sleep (for battery life) is a later optimization. |
| 8 | **What it saves** | Continuous **50 Hz trace of \|a\| while moving** + a per-jump list, on built-in flash. | Re-tunable offline against the same sim we already tested, and it captures **missed** jumps too (a plain per-jump clip wouldn't). Memory card is the escape hatch if flash runs short. |
| 9 | **Waterproofing** | Bought screw-top waterproof **capsule**. Bucket-test empty every time. Must float + be tethered. | Buying beats building for speed; one purchase settles waterproofing, charging, data offload, and storage at once. |
| 10 | **Mounting** | Adhesive **GoPro-style mount**, hard smooth patch of center deck. Alcohol-prep, 24 h cure, tethered. | Center = cleanest signal. Adhesive = no drilling. Tether + float because a lost puck is the classic ending. |
| 11 | **Test plan** | **Desk → dry land (filmed) → water (filmed).** | Each step fails cheaply and catches its own class of bug *before* the ocean, where mistakes are expensive. |

## Added for the "hardware is my only job" build-out

| # | Decision | Choice | Why |
|---|----------|--------|-----|
| 12 | **One-command tooling** | Everything software-side runs through `./tools/jump` (flash, self-test, desk test, calibration, session sync, `eval` — score the detector over labeled sessions, [docs/data-pipeline.md](docs/data-pipeline.md)). | The builder's job is hardware only; each step of the runbook is a single command with PASS/FAIL output and fix hints. The desk-phase scoring loop is done; the downstream gate is the first labeled water session. |
| 13 | **Raw MPU-6050 driver** | Minimal register-level driver instead of the Adafruit library **(ESP32/MPU-6050 build; the accel-only stance here is scoped to this build — see #29)**. | Cheap Amazon clone chips often report an unexpected WHO_AM_I and the popular libraries refuse to start. Ours warns and continues — what matters is the accel actually reading ~1 g, which the self-test checks directly. |
| 14 | **Power-on self-test** | Firmware checks I2C, chip ID, gravity, noise floor, and storage at every boot, with plain-English fix hints; a wiring failure never bricks the session (`selftest` re-probes without re-flashing). | "Did I wire it right?" gets answered in 5 seconds, by the device itself. |
| 15 | **Single source of truth for settings** | `config/params.json` drives the firmware (via a generated header), the simulator, and the analysis. | Eliminates the "tuned Python but forgot the C++" failure mode entirely. |
| 16 | **Bench calibration by measured drops** | `./tools/jump drop`: physics fixes the free-fall time of a measured drop exactly, so timing bias is measured and corrected as an additive `airtime_offset_s` — no video needed on the bench. | Detection latency is constant in *time*, so an additive correction generalizes across jump sizes better than a height multiplier. Video ground truth remains for the on-water check (`height_scale`). |
| 17 | **Fake device for rehearsal + CI** | `tools/fake_device.py` emulates the firmware's serial protocol on a pty; every CLI flow runs against it (`--fake`) and the whole stack is integration-tested by `./tools/jump simtest`. | The entire test/calibration experience can be rehearsed before the hardware exists, and every software change is regression-tested without a board on the desk. |
| 18 | **Wizard + diagnostic bundle** | `./tools/jump wizard` is the front door: one resumable guided flow (software → find board → flash → desk test → calibrate) with a ✅ or a concrete fix at every step. Every command logs its terminal output *and* raw serial traffic to `data/logs/`; `./tools/jump report` bundles system info, config, wizard progress, a live device self-test, and recent logs into one file to hand to Claude. | The builder experience should be "plug it into the MacBook and follow along" — and when something does go wrong, the evidence is already collected, so remote troubleshooting starts from data instead of memory. |

## Added in Phase 3 (live stats + browser app + zero-install flashing)

| # | Decision | Choice | Why |
|---|----------|--------|-----|
| 19 | **BLE = the same protocol, wireless** | The firmware mirrors its exact serial line protocol over a Nordic UART Service (NimBLE stack, not the stock Bluedroid one), device name `JumpHeight`. A phone subscribes and sees the very same `JUMP ...` lines the CLI does. | One protocol everywhere — CLI, fake device, web, BLE — means nothing can drift, and every existing tool/test stays valid. NimBLE because Bluedroid's flash/RAM footprint wouldn't leave room for a big log partition. |
| 20 | **Browser app, no build step** | `web/` is one static vanilla-JS page: connect over Web Bluetooth (live jumps) or Web Serial (self-test, session download → history + CSV), plus an ESP Web Tools install button for in-browser flashing. Served locally by `./tools/jump web` (localhost is a secure context) or by GitHub Pages. | Zero install for the user, zero build tooling for contributors, testable end-to-end in CI (Playwright drives the real page against a mock device). iPhone caveat: iOS Safari has no Web Bluetooth — use the free Bluefy browser. |
| 21 | **Partition map + CI binaries** | Custom no-OTA partition table (app 1.5 MB, logs 2.4 MB — trace cap raised to ~45 min). Flasher binaries are **not** committed: `./tools/jump web` stages them from a local build, and the GitHub Action builds them fresh and publishes `web/` + binaries to Pages. | No OTA slots needed when flashing is over USB/web; committing binaries would bloat and stale the repo. **Upgrade note:** the new partition table reformats stored data on first boot — `sync` before upgrading a device that has sessions on it. |

## Added during hardware bring-up (the field-debug week)

| # | Decision | Choice | Why |
|---|----------|--------|-----|
| 22 | **Calibrate to the unit, at runtime** | Every boot, the self-test measures resting gravity and normalizes all motion math to it. The two calibration terms (`airtime_offset_s`, `height_scale`) live in device memory (NVS), settable over BLE/serial (`set airtime_offset_s …`), surviving reboot *and* reflash. | A real field sensor read 0.824 g at rest — mis-scaled, not broken; normalization makes any unit usable. Device-resident calibration makes a phone a complete calibration tool (no reflash), and is the stated prerequisite for the future sealed/potted build. |
| 23 | **Test it untethered, the way it's used** | Desk test and drop calibration run with the cable unplugged: baseline the stored jump list → act → replug → read the results back. The web app carries phone-only versions (toss test + drop calibration) over BLE, saving calibration straight to the device. | Yanked USB plugs killed test after test — the device is a logger, so its tests should be too. The phone-only flows are the beach workflow: the cable's remaining jobs are first flash, upgrades, charging, and fastest bulk sync. |

## The v2 pivot

| # | Decision | Choice | Why |
|---|----------|--------|-----|
| 24 | **All-in on the XIAO nRF52840 Sense as the v2 board** *(2026-07-28)* | v2 targets the thumb-sized Sense (onboard LSM6DS3TR-C IMU, 2 MB flash, BLE-only, 500 mAh cell): a tiny "Garmin data-field adder" that streams live to watch + phone, with new measurements layered on as new protocol keys. Port spec + gap analysis: [docs/sense.md](docs/sense.md) — headline consequences: BLE layer rewrite (NimBLE→Bluefruit), binary trace v2 becomes launch-blocking (2 MB CSV ≈ 45 min; binary ≈ 5 h), Nordic DFU replaces the ESP32 OTA plan, battery telemetry + System OFF sleep get built for the first time. | Smallest + most efficient is the owner's stated north star, and every client already speaks the chip-neutral protocol (NUS is *native* on nRF52 — Bluefy, web app, Garmin field connect unchanged). The FireBeetle rig keeps water-day duty until the Sense passes the identical bench → bucket → water gauntlet: measurement continuity over novelty. |
| 25 | **Sense records at ±16 g (ESP32 stays ±8 g)** | The Sense build's LSM6DS3TR-C ships at ±16 g (0.488 mg/LSB) from day one; the ESP32/MPU-6050 build stays at ±8 g (0.244 mg/LSB) — a deliberate per-platform divergence, not a global change. | Real board-sport landings peak 4.2–5.5+ g and marine impact literature runs 7–10 g+ ([docs/research.md](docs/research.md) §2/§6); detection thresholds (0.35 g / 2.5 g) are unaffected either way; the binary trace's u16 milli-g format was given ±16 g headroom by design. Commit 1b2c337. |
| 26 | **Host torture-harness before hardware** | The real, unmodified `jh_store.cpp` compiled against a RAM-backed mock of the QSPI flash API with real NOR semantics (erase=0xFF, write can only clear bits) plus power-cut fault injection (`firmware/test/store_host/`) — run before any Sense board existed. | Found and fixed two real bugs pre-silicon: a torn write that permanently wedged the append offset, and a full flag that never tripped near the end of the region. Commit 40afbac. The adversarial review then hardened it further. |
| 27 | **Prune the ESP32-era future; feature-freeze the FireBeetle** *(2026-07-29)* | Deleted the unused `esp32dev` build env; retired Phase 3.5 (WiFi sync, unbuilt) and the ESP32 OTA spec (ota.md → tombstone; full text in git history ≤ `bf4d2aa`). The FireBeetle build is **bugfix-only** from here: it flies water day and any session until the Sense passes the same desk → drop-cal → bucket → water gauntlet, then retires to spare. Named for post-takeover retirement (not yet): the ESP Web Tools browser flasher + its CI staging, BUILD.md's ESP32 wiring/flash sections (freeze as v1 appendix), the `firebeetle32` env itself (keep building in CI until the day it's cut). | Every line kept buildable is a line someone must keep true. The all-in Sense decision (#24) made the WiFi/esp_ota future dead weight — recording the cut (and where the specs live in history) costs less than quietly maintaining fiction. |

## Added during the desk-simulation phase

| # | Decision | Choice | Why |
|---|----------|--------|-----|
| 28 | **Airtime method validated for wings by simulation** *(2026-08)* | Wing jumps are **ballistic**: `h = g·T²÷8` holds. Monte-Carlo (5000 jumps) gives overshoot **1.00–1.07×** (mean 1.013×, p99 1.064×) — versus the **kite exception at 2.31×** — and a method physics-floor **RMSE of 4.2 cm**. *(Rerun at **200,000 jumps** 2026-08-11: bulk numbers hold — mean 1.0128×, p99 1.0622× — RMSE refines to **4.6 cm**, and the `>1.20×` tail disappears. Two corrections: the original "100% detected" was undersampling, and the decision stands anyway. See #30.)* The arm-force ceiling caps mid-air vertical lift well below a kite's, which is what keeps flight near-ballistic. Sim-answered; pending real-water confirmation. Full study: [docs/wing-ballistic-sim.md](docs/wing-ballistic-sim.md). | This closes #1's *real unknown* — "does a wing add lift mid-air?" was previously an open question with zero literature. Simulation de-risks it before any water day: the airtime method ships, and the on-water measurement now confirms rather than discovers. |
| 29 | **Gyro is a detector hot-path requirement (spun jumps)** | On the Sense build, a raw **accel-only** detector *fails* on spinning jumps: at ~300 dps peak the spin holds \|a\| above the 0.35 g free-fall gate (re-pinning takeoff and truncating airtime), and by ~518 dps it trips the 2.5 g false-landing test — height error runs −80…−97%. The fix is **per-sample ω²r subtraction inlined into `jump_detector.h`** ahead of the gates, which requires a **lever-arm / mount calibration**. Gyro = **LSM6DS3TR-C** (Sense's onboard 6-axis IMU) at **±2000 dps, ODR ≥208 Hz + digital LPF, ~0.9 mA HP**, with pre-takeoff bias subtraction. Study: [docs/gyro-sim-plan.md](docs/gyro-sim-plan.md) (g4). | The accel-only stance of #4/#13 was scoped to the ESP32/MPU-6050 build; it does not survive spin. Moving ω²r onto the detector hot-path makes the gyro a *powered-and-settled-by-takeoff* input, not a recording-only extra, and confirms the roadmap's Tier-C "detector hardening" work. |
| 30 | **Silent misses are a known, bounded failure mode — say so** *(2026-08-11)* | The E2 Monte Carlo was rerun at **200,000 jumps** (40×). Bulk statistics held (mean overshoot 1.0133→1.0128×, p99 1.0637→1.0622×) and the lone `>1.20×` draw proved to be noise, but the headline **"100% detected" did not survive**: the detector silently drops **5 jumps in 200,000** (2.5e-5), every one in the `constant` never-depower technique (5/6179 = 0.08% of it), at 20.1–21.5 m/s wind and modest apex (1.00–2.36 m). The boundary is exactly the free-fall gate: **all 5 jumps with mid-flight `spec_g` ≥ `freefall_enter_g` (0.35 g) were missed; the one jump between 0.34 and 0.35 g was caught.** No threshold change is made. | E5's failure map had already predicted "hard-blind at a_v ≥ 0.34 g"; at N=5000 the Monte Carlo simply never sampled there (155 `constant` samples), so two independent experiments now agree, and the mechanism is the kite exception in miniature — sustained lift holds `|a|` above the gate, so takeoff never registers. Lowering the gate to chase 2.5e-5 of jumps would trade a rare silent miss for common false takeoffs on chop: a worse failure. The right response is to **document it and let the water day measure real `spec_g`** (sim says p99 0.0667 g, max 0.4414 g; only 0.52% of jumps reach E5's 0.09 g danger band). A silent miss shows nothing on the watch and leaves no log, so it is recorded as a known limit rather than rounded away. Study: [docs/wing-ballistic-sim.md](docs/wing-ballistic-sim.md) E2′. |

## Added during Sense bring-up (the bus-corruption week)

| # | Decision | Choice | Why |
|---|----------|--------|-----|
| 31 | **Non-destructive mount retry: `jh_store::try_mount` / `mount`** *(2026-08-12)* | `try_mount()` runs the same mount ladder as `init()` (plain begin → DPD wake → JEDEC 66/99 reset) but drops the auto-format: a chip that answers the JEDEC probe with an unreadable superblock is announced and refused, never rebuilt, because it may be a wedged **read** returning garbage over otherwise-intact data (the mule's maybe-alive 61-jump history). New `mount` command, StoreGuard-bracketed like boot; `format` remains the caller-chosen destructive path. Torture-tested incl. a byte-for-byte nothing-touched assertion over a corrupt superblock (`tools/tests/test_store_host.py`, 18/18). Commit 9ca48fd. | 16e's mule "wedge" turned out to be self-inflicted (see #34), but the real exposure it surfaced was real: an unreadable superblock must never be treated as an invitation to auto-format, because the read failing tells you nothing about whether the data underneath is fine. Storage recovery doctrine is now retry-non-destructively first, destroy only on explicit say-so (bench-playbook §5). |
| 32 | **off-path back-feed fixed: release the bus before cutting the rail** *(2026-08-12)* | `system_off()` reordered to `Wire1.end()` → SDA/SCL/INT1 to input-no-pull → settle → rail LOW → System OFF (was: rail cut while TWIM still drove the bus). Nordic DevZone, for this exact module: P1.08 enables a small regulator that powers **both** the LSM6DS3TR-C's VDD and the on-module I2C pull-ups; energizing SDA/SCL while that rail is down back-drives the sensor through its pins and corrupts its power-up sequencing — it then never ACKs, indistinguishable from dead silicon. System OFF retains pin state, so every prior soft-off left the sensor unpowered with both lines held at 3.3 V for the whole sleep. Fix shipped but **unflashed**, bench frozen for the morning meter session. Commit 77951ec; mechanism + meter protocol in [firmware/SENSE_FIRST_BOOT.md](firmware/SENSE_FIRST_BOOT.md) §16g. | Two prior verdicts on "why won't the sensor ACK" — a dead-die RCA, then a held-bus clamp theory (§16f) — were both wrong, because the real cause was our own `off` command: proven "both directions" on silicon (54fa232, web button a3e4889) the very day before the mule's sensor stopped ACKing. The mechanism was sitting on Nordic DevZone the entire time either verdict could have searched for it. |
| 33 | **Electrical safety rules (bench-playbook §6b)** *(2026-08-12)* | Five standing rules adopted after #32: (1) no MCU line into a peripheral's power domain may be energized while that domain is down — power-up is float bus → rail up → settle → attach, power-down is detach → float lines → rail down, both sequences now live in code (`jh_imu::revive`, `jh_power::system_off`) and new code **copies them, never improvises**; (2) any code touching a power/rail/bus pin gets a datasheet-or-web sequencing check *before* it reaches silicon; (3) new-board quarantine — a fresh board meets only the current audited build, never experimental electrical code, until a sacrificial board has survived the off/sleep/wake cycle repeatedly; (4) electrical experiments run on the designated sacrificial board only (currently the mule); (5) one flash per session where possible — seven-flash evenings are where sequencing mistakes compound. Commit 576effb. | The #32 hazard was documented and searchable the whole time two wrong theories (dead die, then clamp) were being chased instead — the lesson isn't "look harder next time," it's a rule that removes the choice. The Puck, a fresh board, ran probe-era rail-cycling code during the falsifier session and was dead within a day: precisely the accident rule 3's quarantine exists to stop from recurring on the next fresh board. |
| 34 | **Liveness probes must be side-effect-free** *(2026-08-12)* | Probe device liveness with `info` or `stats` — never with a command whose designed failure mode is the symptom being hunted. Bench-playbook §2.6. | The mule's "command-silence" mystery ([firmware/SENSE_FIRST_BOOT.md](firmware/SENSE_FIRST_BOOT.md) §16e) was the tester using `selftest` — the documented deliberate hang-and-reboot retry on a ProbeGuarded board — as its liveness check, then logging the reboot it caused as a wedge. `info`/`stats` had answered instantly and completely the whole time; the fix was recognizing the probe itself was the bug, not the device under test. |
| 35 | **A register that hasn't converted yet is not a sample** *(2026-08-13)* | Cold-boot selftest discards reads until the first non-zero accel triple (bounded) before running its noise statistics. With BDU set, OUTX..OUTZ hold reset 0x0000 until the first conversion (≥1/ODR + turn-on) after CTRL1_XL's power-down exit — and the selftest's first read landed <1 ms after that write. One zero triple in N=100 puts sd at 0.0995, past the 0.08 FAIL line: a HEALTHY cold-booted sensor printed `noise FAIL` deterministically. Commit dca2985; found by the pre-flash audit before board #3's first boot, confirmed against the archive's own boot rows (0.960 g/0.0966 = on-command 0.970 g + exactly one zero). | Same doctrine as the SAADC timeout rule (jh_power.cpp): a measurement that did not happen must never be dressed up as a value. This one nearly convicted the third board on arrival — the exact "sensor is bad too" banner that historically triggered the destructive experiments — and it had already left artifacts in the archive that two RCAs stepped around ("filter settle, benign-looking, unverified"). |
| 36 | **The software meter: peripheral pull-ups as a rail indicator, pin readback as a driver check** *(2026-08-13)* | `railcheck` (mule branch): detach the bus, enable internal pull-DOWNS on SDA/SCL, step the rail-enable and read the levels — the module's own I2C pull-ups hang off the switched rail, so HIGH-against-pulldown ⇔ rail up. Read EN back through its own input buffer (INPUT_CONNECT while driving) to separate "MCU can't drive the net" from "rail path dead beyond it"; validate the method on a known-good driven pin on the same board. Pull-downs only — the 16g hazard (energizing an unpowered domain) is structurally impossible in this instrument. | The multimeter session was never going to happen ("I don't have time"), and the answer mattered: it closed the mule's case in three serial commands — `en=1 pin=0`, EN net stuck at ground, hard board fault, die possibly innocent — after two days of theories built on bus-side symptoms alone. When a fault question reduces to "is this net at 0 V or 3.3 V", look for a powered indicator already wired to that net before asking for probes. |
| 37 | **Pins that carry current are configured with explicit drive strength, never `pinMode()`** *(2026-08-14)* | `jh_imu::init()`/`revive()` configure P1.08 via `nrf_gpio_cfg(..., NRF_GPIO_PIN_H0H1, ...)`. Rationale from Seeed's own schematic (v1.1 sheet 2): P1.08 drives no regulator and no FET — the GPIO pad is wired directly to the LSM6DS3TR-C's VDD/VDDIO and to both 10k bus pull-ups, i.e. **the MCU pin IS the sensor's power supply**. Load is ~1.6 mA plus inrush; nRF52840 standard drive (what `pinMode(OUTPUT)` silently selects) is specified at **0.5 mA**, high drive at 5 mA. Under-driven, the pad sags to ground, the rail never rises, the bus sits low, and the sensor is indistinguishable from dead silicon. Commit 859ad42; full RCA in [firmware/SENSE_FIRST_BOOT.md](firmware/SENSE_FIRST_BOOT.md) §16i. | This one bug consumed four days, produced two confident "dead hardware" verdicts, and nearly had a third board written off. Both "dead" boards came back with one register field changed: board #3 `accel PASS 1.022g`, the mule `accel PASS 1.029g`. Nothing was ever damaged. The Arduino idiom is the trap — it looks like the canonical way to drive a pin and quietly picks the weakest driver on the chip. |
| 38 | **A pin-level claim requires control pins measured the same way, in the same pass** *(2026-08-14)* | The `pincensus` command reads EVERY GPIO twice (against an internal pull-down, then a pull-up) and prints the pair. A free pin must read `01`; anything else is a claim about that specific pin. Any future "this pin is stuck" statement must cite a census, not a single reading. | Every instrument that convicted the hardware sampled ONLY the broken domain, so each one inherited the fault and confirmed the wrong story. The census made the answer unmissable in one line: unrelated pins all read `01` (instrument sound) while the whole sensor domain read `00` (load real). Corollary, learned the hard way: reading SDA/SCL against internal pull-downs is *meaningless* here — with 10k pull-ups to the switched rail, a perfectly healthy board reads LOW too, because the divider sits below the input-high threshold. A test that returns the same answer for healthy and broken hardware is not evidence. |
| 39 | **Two failed verdicts on the same evidence means the instrument is suspect, not the hardware** *(2026-08-14)* | When a diagnosis of "dead hardware" fails to hold up twice, stop testing the hardware and start testing the measurement: read the pin back, add control pins, check the driver against a known-good one, and pull the vendor schematic before spending another board. | Stated as doctrine because the project kept re-deriving it: the sensor RCA (08-11), the clamp theory (08-12), the back-feed theory (08-13) and the "EN net stuck at ground" verdict (08-14 morning) were four different hardware stories built on measurements taken inside the fault. The owner's instinct — "I just cannot believe we've really killed the hardware" — was right every single time. |

## Deliberately deferred to later phases

- **Real deep-sleep** power management (multi-session battery life). v1 charges after each outing. *(Also the gate for the potted-puck + solar idea — energy math and prerequisites studied in the roadmap's Phase 4 backlog note.)*
- **Live in-session readout on the water.** BLE + the web app now show jumps live, but 2.4 GHz doesn't travel through water and you can't read a phone mid-wing — live stats are for the beach between runs; the session record remains the product.
- **microSD card** for unlimited logging. v1 uses built-in flash (~45 min of moving-time trace, capped; grew with the Phase 3 partition map).
- **Better IMU / GPS / custom PCB.** All Phase 4.
- **Air-drag / wave-landing correction** beyond a single global factor.

## Known v1 limitations (accepted on purpose)

- Simple motion-gating keeps logs clean but doesn't save power yet → **seal near launch, charge after**.
- Trace logging caps at ~45 min of *moving* time to protect flash; the jump list is always kept.
- A drop off a ledge reads as a "jump" (it's really fall height) — fine for this use.
