# Audit 2026-08-22 — what is still open

The audit ran 21 tickets (F-01…F-21) across build gates, power, storage
integrity, the watch, BLE pinning, sim/firmware parity and test rigor. **All 21
are closed**, each one mutation-tested, and the OG was flashed and
desk-verified at `src=e83f6395`.

The per-ticket detail — 1,000 lines of it — was cut on 2026-08-23. It was a
record of finished work, and the 21 commit hashes it cited are the real record.
Read them with `git log`, or restore the full ledger:

```
git show archive/docs-2026-08-23:docs/audit-2026-08-22.md
```

Two of the audit's own premises were disproven by measurement while working it,
which is worth remembering before trusting any audit document, including this
one: F-08's prescribed fix would not have met its own acceptance criterion, and
F-18's "constants are triplicated" was wrong — the watch bounds differ on
purpose.

What survives below is the part that is still load-bearing: **the findings that
are open**, and **the theories that were investigated and killed**, so nobody
spends a second evening re-chasing them.

### [ ] F-24 · minor · hand-verified — self-arm cannot bootstrap at a small lever arm

- **Location:** `sim/lever_arm.py` + `firmware/src/main.cpp:1634-1660` (the
  commit gate); exposed by `tools/tests/test_lever_arm.py`'s r=0.2 case
- **Defect:** the lever-arm estimator commits only on a VALIDATED jump, but at
  a small lever arm and high spin the flight that carries the observations is
  the one the detector REJECTS. Traced sample by sample at r=0.2 m / 600 dps,
  uncalibrated:

      t=2.925   64 observations   REJECTED    -> discard
      t=3.560    0 observations   validated   -> commit, with nothing to commit

  So the estimator never bootstraps. Larger radii work because their first
  AIRBORNE exit is itself validated.
- **Evidence:** `test_uncalibrated_device_fixes_itself_over_jumps[0.2]`, now
  `xfail(strict=True)`. It passed until F-17 only because the test was running
  the retired ungated commit policy — the same policy that corrupted
  calibration ~50×. There is no version of this that is both safe and passing
  today.
- **NOT REACHABLE ON THE DEVICE:** `JH_SPIN_SELFARM_ENABLED` is 0 — self-arm is
  compiled out for the one-shot water session. This is a design gap in a
  feature that is currently off, not a live defect.
- **Fix (not costed):** the estimator needs a bootstrap path that does not
  depend on a validated jump — e.g. accept observations from a flight rejected
  *for airtime reasons only* while still refusing the railed-gyro phantoms that
  motivated the gate. Distinguishing those two rejects is the actual work.
- **Do NOT fix by reverting the gate.** That is the ~50× corruption path, and
  the strict xfail exists so that reverting it announces itself.

### [ ] F-22 · minor · hand-verified — trace_bytes() over-reports once the region fills

- **Location:** `firmware/src/platform/nrf52/jh_store.cpp` — `trace_append()`
  accounting vs `walkTraceRegion()`
- **Defect:** the live counter adds each sample's CSV length as text arrives,
  but samples sit in an open block until it closes. Ordinarily that is a lag
  the next block write settles. Once the region is FULL it becomes permanent:
  `closeAndWriteBlock()` drops a block that no longer fits, and the bytes it
  held stay counted.
- **Evidence:** filling the region in the harness gives `TRACE_BYTES
  n=14476006` live, but a remount recomputes 14,475,206 — 800 bytes, exactly
  one 50-sample batch at 16 B/line. `TRACE_RECHECK` agrees with the remount, so
  the *stored* side is self-consistent; the live counter is the one that drifts.
- **Impact:** display only, and it self-corrects at the next boot. It reaches a
  rider as a `trace_bytes` in STATS slightly ahead of what a `dump` produces,
  at the one moment (a full region) they are most likely to compare the two.
- **Fix:** settle the counter against what was actually written when a block is
  dropped, or state in `jh_store.h` that `trace_bytes()` is an upper bound and
  make `tracecheck` the authority. Do NOT fix by making appends count only at
  block close — that would make the number lag ~5 s behind during a ride.
- **Found by:** F-08's parity work. Nothing else compares the two counters.
- **Second board, 2026-09-10:** the Puck (`15b2d468`), region filled with
  `fillstore` to `region_full=1`, reported `trace_bytes=14,093,819` and
  delivered a **14,093,804 B** body — **−15 B**. The OG's was **−765 B**. Two
  boards, two different deltas, both negative and both inside the 1..800 B
  band, which is now resting on more than one observation. Note also that the
  two full regions report CSV-equivalent totals ~13 % apart (14.09 MB vs
  15.92 MB) on identical 2,027,520 B physical regions — `trace_bytes` at
  exhaustion is content-dependent, so no fixed-MB comparison can stand in for
  this check.
- **Seen live 2026-09-07, on the OG, region full:** `tracecheck fast=15917918
  slow=15917153` (−765 B). `./tools/jump sync` verifies its download against
  the STATS `trace_bytes` — the fast number — and printed `TRACE INCOMPLETE …
  (-765) … Do NOT clear` for a byte-complete file, twice, 7 min apart. The
  impact is no longer "display only": **the sync tool refuses to clear a
  correctly-downloaded full region**, which is exactly the state a puck is in
  when a clear matters most. Fix on the tool side: on a byte mismatch, issue
  `tracecheck` and compare against `slow` before declaring the file short.

### [ ] F-23 · minor · hand-verified — full-chip mount is ~80× empty, and no counter scheme fixes it

- **Location:** `firmware/src/platform/nrf52/jh_store.cpp` — `walkTraceRegion()`
- **Defect:** F-08's acceptance asked for full-chip mount within ~10× of
  empty. After F-08 it is 74 ms vs 0.93 ms. The residue is not accounting: a
  floor probe with the counter replaced by a no-op still took 60 ms. The scan
  reads and CRC-checks every block in the region to find the append point.
- **Why the ticket's own fix would not have helped:** persisting cumulative
  totals in block headers removes the counting, not the walking. The walk is
  the floor.
- **Fix (real one, not costed):** find the erase boundary without decoding
  every block — a binary search over the region for the first erased page
  would be ~16 reads instead of ~19,000 blocks. This CONFLICTS with the
  deliberate torn-write behaviour of continuing past a damaged patch (a prior
  boot may have resumed appending beyond it), so it needs a design decision
  about which guarantee wins, not a local edit.
- **Impact:** bounded and already survivable — the scan feeds the watchdog
  every 64 blocks (a fix that predates this audit). It is boot latency on a
  well-used puck, not a failure.

### [x] F-25 · minor · CLOSED 2026-08-24 — `jump status` reports the help text and calls it the binary

> Both fix halves shipped: the tool label + dispatch-table diff on 08-23, and
> the help string itself in `42dbd59`, flashed to the OG the same night
> (`src=76df4a83`). `jump status` reads `commands advertised by help (21)`
> with no warning line. Original entry below, unchanged:

- **Location:** `tools/jump:2563-2567`; `firmware/src/main.cpp:613`
- **Defect:** `./tools/jump status` prints `commands in binary (N): ...`, but
  it derives that list by running `strings` over the ELF and taking the line
  beginning `# commands:` — which is the **help string**, not the dispatch
  table. Anything dispatched but not advertised is invisible to it. Today
  three commands are: `pincensus` (`main.cpp:735`), `gyro` (`main.cpp:1145`)
  and `vbatscan` (`main.cpp:1183`). So the tool says 18 where the binary
  handles 21.
- **Why it is not cosmetic:** the label makes a claim about the *binary*
  while the evidence supports only a claim about the *help text*, under a
  banner that reads "machine-checked; anything not checkable is marked
  UNKNOWN, never assumed." The specific casualty is `pincensus`: DECISION #38
  makes a census mandatory for any "this pin is stuck" statement, and a
  reader who checks availability the documented way concludes the instrument
  was never shipped. That is the 2026-08-14 shape again — the fact is present
  and correct, and nothing joins it to the screen.
- **Evidence:** `./tools/jump status` on the OG tonight printed
  `commands in binary (18)` with `pincensus`, `gyro` and `vbatscan` absent,
  against three live `else if (cmd == ...)` arms at the lines above.
- **Fix:** two halves, and the cheap one is the important one.
  1. **Relabel, no firmware change:** call it `commands advertised by help
     (N)`, so the claim matches the evidence. Free, correct today, needs no
     flash. Better still, parse the dispatch arms out of `main.cpp` and print
     the *difference* — an undocumented command is itself a finding.
  2. **Add the three to `main.cpp:613`** — but this touches silicon, so it
     rides the next flash batch and never triggers one of its own (CLAUDE.md
     rule 4).
- **Do NOT fix** by deleting the three commands to make the list true. They
  are working diagnostics; `pincensus` is doctrine.
- **Found by:** the 2026-08-23 documentation sweep, while establishing which
  tool outputs the docs were allowed to cite as evidence.

### [ ] F-28 · minor · field-measured — phantoms self-identify in a column the firmware computes and ignores

- **Found:** 2026-08-29, first real field exposure (49 h pocket-carry incl. a
  5-mile run). 4 phantom detections in ~4.4 h of motion = **0.9/h, inside
  the <1/h budget by a hair** — and every phantom carries
  `med_acorr_g` 0.64–1.40 while all 6 deliberate tosses sit at 0.04–0.26.
  A **2.5× separation**, in a per-jump column the firmware already computes
  and stores (`jumps.csv` med columns) but does not act on.
- **Candidate fix:** a live median-airborne-|a| gate at ~0.5 g would have
  killed 4/4 phantoms and kept 6/6 real events in this dataset. It is the
  offline `selfdiag` flag, promoted to the hot path.
- **Do NOT ship before the water day.** One field session is one dataset;
  a wing jump with sustained lift legitimately reads elevated med-|a|
  (DECISION #30's band), and an over-eager gate here is exactly how real
  jumps get silently eaten (E11's lesson). Water-day data first, then
  decide with both distributions in hand.
- ~~**Until then:** the phantoms are short (≤0.4 s), small (≤0.21 m), and
  post-filterable.~~ **Retracted 2026-09-06.** Water session, vest mount:
  phantom #9 (a water-entry fall) read **0.78 s / 0.75 m** at `med_a` 0.50 g
  and became the watch's displayed best airtime. Phantoms are not reliably
  small. The separation itself held: 6 phantoms 0.502–1.533 g, 9 tosses
  0.036–0.231 g (`data/sessions/20260906-192422/jumps.csv`). Still no
  real water jump in either distribution, so the do-not-ship rule stands.
- **Pooled corpus 2026-09-06, and a lesson about how to pool it.** A first
  pass bucketed detections by time-window and got "16 tosses 0.036–0.255 g
  vs 11 phantoms 0.502–1.533 g, a 0.247 g gap". **That was wrong**: the
  window heuristic swallowed a spurious detection into the toss set. Scoring
  against `labels.csv` with strict 1:1 matching — the same truth `jump eval`
  uses — gives **15 real tosses 0.036–0.231 g against 12 spurious
  0.255–1.533 g, a margin of 0.024 g.** The gap-closer is 08-29
  `t=116992.578`, 0.534 s / 0.35 m / **0.255 g**, firing 0.5 s after
  labelled toss 2/3: a bounce inside the toss burst. A gate at 0.24–0.25 g
  kills 12/12 spurious and eats 0/15 real; the 0.5 g gate under discussion
  kills 11/12 and misses that bounce.
- **CORRECTED AGAIN, same evening, by the adversarial verifier in the
  analysis run** — and this is the number to quote. The label-strict pool
  used only the two sessions carrying a `labels.csv` and therefore ignored
  the bench sessions whose deliberate events are documented elsewhere:
  `20260824-183054` (18 tosses, the drop-calibration evening; its own drop
  log at `data/logs/20260824-182904-drop.log:41` receives n=18 verbatim and
  the run's diagnostic calls those events "valid-looking jump … should have
  counted") and `20260827-122115` (8 drops, airtimes 0.441–0.471 s, heights
  0.238–0.272 m — unmistakably one calibration set). Pooling those plus the
  lone unlabelled 09-05 detection gives **41 real vs 13 spurious, highest
  real 0.238 g (08-24 n=18) against lowest spurious 0.255 g (08-29 n=2):
  a 17 mg margin, and a separating window of 0.239–0.2549 g.**
  **The separation is complete and the window is 17 mg wide. There is no
  engineering headroom here at all.**
- **THE MECHANISTIC REASON NOT TO SHIP IT** (new, and stronger than the
  small-sample caution above). `med_acorr_g` equals `med_a_g` on every row
  of every session, because `spin_lever_m` is 0.0 and
  `JH_SPIN_SELFARM_ENABLED = 0` (F-24) — **the ω²r correction has never
  once been applied to real data.** `algorithm.md:188-190` says the median
  separates ballistic from non-ballistic at AUC 1.0 *without* spin and
  "under spin it needs the gyro". Uncorrected, ω²r for a sensor 0.5 m from
  the rotation axis at 180 °/s is **0.50 g — exactly the proposed gate**;
  at 270 °/s it is 1.13 g, deep inside the phantom band.
- **And the toss corpus cannot bound that risk.** The tosses rotate FAST
  (243–783 °/s median) yet read LOW (≤0.255 g), because a tumbling board
  in a vest spins about an axis close to the sensor — a small lever arm.
  A rider-plus-board rotates about the rider's mass centre, far from a
  board-mounted sensor: lower rate, much larger arm. **So the corpus that
  makes the gate look safe is the one configuration in which spin
  contamination is smallest.** Ship the gate and a rotating jump is the
  thing it eats. Arm the ω²r correction FIRST, or gate on something else.
- **The two facts together are the whole argument.** The usable margin is
  24 mg; uncorrected ω²r contributes 0.03 g at 90 °/s on a 0.1 m arm and
  0.50 g at 180 °/s on a 0.5 m arm. **The spin term is one to thirty times
  the entire 17 mg window.** No amount of extra bench tossing narrows this,
  because tossing is the configuration with the smallest arm. The blocker
  is not sample size — it is that the correction which makes the feature
  meaningful has never been armed on real data (F-24).

### [x] F-30 · REFUTED 2026-09-06, same day it was filed — nothing was lost; the two counters measure different spans

- **Filed** on 2026-09-06 from `session_jumps=16 stored_jumps=15`, on the
  assumption that a jump was detected, counted, transmitted and then
  silently refused by the store. **That assumption was wrong.** Filing it
  before checking what resets each counter is the fifth wrong verdict in
  this project's ledger, and the first one filed by the same agent that
  then refuted it within the hour.
- **What the code actually says:** `session_jumps` is declared at
  `main.cpp:134` and that is its ONLY assignment — no reset exists, in
  `clear`, at activity boundaries, or anywhere else. It counts every
  detection since the last PUCK BOOT. `clear` zeroes `stored_jumps` and
  `stored_best` only (`main.cpp:736`).
- **The arithmetic, measured:** `data/sessions/20260905-103400/session.json`
  and `20260906-192422/session.json` carry trace epochs 2.7 s apart — the
  SAME boot (09-04 11:51). The 09-05 session stored 1 jump at its
  `takeoff_s=41469` (09-04 23:22); that jump is absent from today's dump,
  and today's trace begins at `t=96243.6`, 76 s after the 09-05 sync read
  uptime 96167.7 — so a clear ran right after that sync. **1 pre-clear +
  15 post-clear = 16.** Today's store indices are contiguous 1..15, as
  expected when no append ever failed.
- **The finding that survives, and it is a product one:** the number on the
  rider's wrist is `session_jumps`, scoped to the last puck reboot. It
  survives `clear`, survives the end of an activity, and survives the day.
  A rider who does not reboot sees yesterday's tosses in today's count —
  which is exactly what happened today, and exactly what the session
  card's reboot ritual exists to prevent. The card asserted the rule; this
  is the mechanism behind it.
- **Fix candidates (decide, do not just patch):** (a) zero `session_jumps`
  in `clear` so the two counters share a span; (b) leave the firmware and
  make the watch display reconcile to `stored_jumps`; (c) neither, and
  document that the count is boot-scoped. Option (a) is one line but
  changes what the rider sees mid-session if a clear ever runs then.
- **Worth doing regardless:** `logJump()` still returns false with no
  emitted line when `fs_ok` is false. It did not fire today, but a silent
  refusal path is exactly what CLAUDE.md rule 3 forbids. Emit a line, and
  add a `store_refused=` STATS key so any future divergence is visible
  without a two-file diff.
- **Why silent:** `logJump()` opens with `if (!fs_ok) return false;` — no
  line emitted. The refusal messages that follow cover only the
  `AppendResult` codes, and even those print once per boot
  (`store_refusal_reported`). Nothing recorded which jump, when, or why.
  The trace spans 09-04 → 09-06 unbroken, so it was not an auto trace
  clear; `fs_ok` had to be false, or the append failed, for one event.
- **Consequence:** the rider's count and the archive disagree by one and
  no one can say which jump is missing — CLAUDE.md §2 rule 3 in firmware.
- **Fix candidates:** emit a `# jump NOT saved (fs down)` line on the
  `!fs_ok` return; count refusals in a `store_refused=` STATS key so the
  discrepancy is visible without a diff. Cause of the one refusal: not
  established; reproduce before theorising.

### [ ] F-36 · MAJOR · measured — picking the puck up to sync it is what destroys the ride

- **Measured 2026-09-11.** The rider's 1 h 54 m session of 2026-09-10 came back
  with **20 jump records and 133 KB of trace** — 166 seconds, all of it recorded
  the next morning **while he was handling the puck to sync it**. Not one of the
  20 stored takeoffs falls inside the surviving trace. The entire raw record of
  the session is gone.
- **Mechanism, exact:** `firmware/src/main.cpp:1733` —
  `moved_now && motion_seen && idle_ms >= AUTO_CLEAR_IDLE_MS && trace_is_full`
  → `trace_clear()`. The region filled during the ride (it began at 9.7 MB of a
  ~15.9 MB capacity and a 1 h 54 m session adds ~5.8 MB at the field-measured
  3.05 MB/h). It then sat overnight — satisfying the 1 h idle — and the **first
  motion after that idle was him picking it up to plug it in.** The auto-clear
  fired at that instant and wiped the trace it was about to hand over.
- **The comment above it states the intended trade** — "OLD raw data is
  sacrificed so the CURRENT session records… Losing the session you are actually
  riding is worse than losing one you already had a chance to sync." The trade is
  defensible. What is not is that **the trigger cannot tell a new session from
  the act of retrieval.** Any motion after an hour's rest looks identical.
- **Impact:** the richest data this project can collect is destroyed by the one
  action guaranteed to precede every attempt to collect it. It is silent — the
  chatter goes to a link nobody is attached to — and the rider sees a normal,
  verified sync.
- **Contributing, and owned:** the page's "Empty the puck" step had been hidden
  from the rider on 2026-09-09, and the owner's `?allowclear=1` admin path
  demanded a redundant multi-MB pull-and-send before it would erase a file he
  already held. So the puck was left full, which is the precondition the
  auto-clear needs. Both are fixed in page `2026-09-11a`: step 4 is offered to
  the rider once a ride is verified AND delivered, and the owner flag lifts the
  delivered requirement.
- **Fix (firmware, next flash batch — not yet done):** do not auto-clear while
  USB is attached, or require sustained motion rather than a single sample, or
  defer the clear until recording actually begins. Any of the three breaks the
  retrieval case without touching the intended one.
- **Found by:** the second real session a rider ever sent home.

### [ ] F-35 · MAJOR · measured — there is no free fall in 85 minutes of real riding

- **Measured on the first real rider trace** (`data/sessions/20260910-103108-E2C4`,
  2026-09-09, 85 min, vest pocket). The ride window is **257,050 samples and
  perfectly gapless** — every dt exactly 0.0200 s, verified independently twice.
- **The longest unbroken run below the 0.35 g free-fall gate, anywhere in boot 2,
  is 13 samples = 0.26 s.** Below 0.5 g: 15 samples. Below 0.7 g: 39 samples
  (0.78 s). A 1.8 m jump — Surfr's own figure for this session — needs
  **1.21 s**, i.e. 60 consecutive samples. A 1.57 s airtime needs ~1.47 s.
  Nothing of that shape exists.
- **Threshold-free confirmation, needing no gate at all:** the minimum mean |a|
  over ANY 1.47 s window in the ride is **0.8367 g**. Free flight would be
  ~0–0.07 g. Detection #7's own window means **1.12 g, the ~98th percentile of
  all 256,976 windows** — the firmware called one of the most heavily *loaded*
  1.5 s stretches of the afternoon "free flight". (The forensics reported 1.19 g
  / 99.24th using a slightly different window alignment; both land in the top
  2 %, and the conclusion does not depend on which.)
- **The likely physical reason, and it is not a bug:** a wing rider hangs from a
  wing that is still generating lift. A chest-worn sensor on a rider suspended
  under a wing has no reason to read 0 g at all. **The free-fall premise the
  whole detector rests on may simply not transfer from a tossed puck, or a board
  mount, to a body-worn sensor on a wing rider.**
- **Impact:** this is upstream of every gate-tuning question. If a wing jump does
  not produce free fall at the chest, no `freefall_enter_g` is the right one and
  E11/E12's 400,000 simulated jumps were answering a question this mount does not
  pose. It does NOT condemn a board mount, which has never been tried.
- **What would settle it:** one session with per-jump ground truth (video, or the
  rider calling times) and, ideally, the same jumps recorded at both mounts.
- **Found by:** six-way forensics on the first bundle a rider ever sent home.

## Refuted — do not re-chase

Raised, investigated, and killed by skeptics. Listed so agents don't
rediscover them:

- **main.cpp:1500 "double-counted jumps"** — mechanism real (unconditional
  increment), but the jumps list re-derives from flash; user-facing counts
  self-correct. Residual interface debt is F-10.
- **JumpFieldView.mc draw-loop allocation / font re-fitting** — refuted on
  cost: allocation volume is within Monkey C GC noise at 1 Hz; no retained
  growth (data layer audited at zero bytes/jump).
- **PuckLink.mc:314 puckName-inert / UUID-first matching** — mechanism
  accurate but intended: UUID-in-advert is the firmware's design; RSSI
  selection among own pucks acceptable. The timeout gap at :186 (F-12) is
  the real finding.
- **sim/windows.py Goertzel & delta-sort "O(n²)"** — reproduces, but runs on
  ~4,000-sample bench traces: milliseconds. Not worth churn.
- **trace_codec.h:156 milli_g rounding divergence C++ vs Python** — real
  divergence (32,765 float32 inputs), but every reachable input is clamped
  upstream; bit-parity holds on the reachable domain.
- **lsm6ds3_min.h two-burst read → one 12-byte burst** — contiguous registers
  confirmed, but the measured saving doesn't clear the noise floor and the
  fix breaks the accel-only fallback path.
- **"unittest discover misses tests" as a CI problem** — CI runs pytest and

- **tools/jump:2481 nine BLE scans in `boards`** — real but bench-only
  wall-clock; the data-integrity half (F-15) is what survived.
