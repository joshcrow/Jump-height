# Prior-art brief: jump-height estimation for a wing-foil IMU puck

*Compiled 2026-09-14. Every claim is tagged **[stated]** (the source says it) or **[inference]** (mine). Nothing below touched the repo.*

## 1. Commercial products

**WOO Sports (kite/wing board-mounted puck).** WOO's own product page lists no sensor complement, no sampling rate, and no accuracy figure — only "extremely precise sensor" and "jump height, airtime, jump graph" ([woosports.com](https://www.woosports.com/en/us/products/woo-woo-kiteboard-sensor-package-4-0-2024-65240-7200)) **[stated — i.e. the vendor spec is marketing-only]**. The community-sourced account is a 9-axis IMU (accel + gyro + mag) fused in a Kalman/Madgwick/Mahony-type filter to get orientation, then **double integration of vertical acceleration** to get the trajectory ([kiteforum thread "This is how WOO is measuring jump height"](https://kiteforum.com/viewtopic.php?t=2418332)) **[stated by the forum, not by WOO — treat as unverified]**. No barometer is documented anywhere I found **[unknown]**.

**Independent validation — the one real study.** Videogrammetric verification, *Sensors* 21(24):8353 ([MDPI](https://www.mdpi.com/1424-8220/21/24/8353) / [PMC8706814](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8706814/)): 20 kite jumps, 3.07–7.30 m, ground truth = four synchronised Nikon D7500 cameras with coded targets on the board plus total-station control, reference accuracy 0.03–0.09 m. Results **[stated]**:

| System | Overestimates | RMS error | Max error |
|---|---|---|---|
| Surfr (iPhone SE) | 75% of jumps | 0.51 m | 1.42 m |
| WOO3 | 90% | 0.70 m | 1.44 m |
| WOO2 | 95% | 0.95 m | 1.90 m |

Above 5 m, deviations reach 15–20% of true height (WOO2 up to 25%) **[stated]**. All three systems overestimate, and error grows with height — consistent with integration drift **[stated: the paper attributes it to accumulating systematic error]**. Critically, the paper does **not** address kite lift or non-ballistic flight at all **[stated absence]**. PIQ and Xensr are named as existing but were not tested **[stated]**.

**Surfr (phone/watch).** Requires "a gyroscope and an accelerometer that provides 100 measurements per second (100Hz)"; 50 Hz devices are explicitly rejected as insufficient for live jump height ([Surfr support](https://support.thesurfr.app/en/articles/9859609-which-devices-are-compatible-with-the-surfr-app)) **[stated]**. That is a hard data point against your 26–50 Hz puck **[inference]**. On Garmin, Surfr runs two paths: an on-watch live calculation with airtime capped at 10 s, and "Surfr.AI" post-session recalculation from raw sensor data with no cap and heliloops handled properly ([thesurfr.app/garmin](https://www.thesurfr.app/garmin)) **[stated]**. They concede rotations confuse wrist sensors **[stated]**. No published accuracy claim of their own **[stated absence]**; the only third-party number is the 0.51 m RMS above.

**PIQ / North Kiteboarding.** 10 g board-mounted tracker, "13 axes" analysed simultaneously by a proprietary algorithm, real-time on-board display ([VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/business/north-kiteboarding-the-worlds-leading-kiteboarding-brand-and-piq-tell-you-the-height-of-your-jump-directly-from-any-kiteboard/), [TheKiteMag](https://www.thekitemag.com/news/north-piq-tracker/)). No algorithm disclosure, no validation **[marketing-only]**.

**Garmin native.** Garmin's watch altitude comes from a blend of GPS and barometric pressure, with no way to force GPS-only, and users report the altitude channel is "practically random" with no correlation to actual jumps in watersports — repeated water immersion destroys the baro ([Garmin forums, Epix Gen 2](https://forums.garmin.com/outdoor-recreation/outdoor-recreation/f/epix-2/304527/altitude-errors-on-water-sport-activities); [kiteforum](https://kiteforum.com/viewtopic.php?t=2414585)) **[stated by users, not Garmin]**. I found **no** evidence Garmin ships a validated native jump-height metric for kite/wing **[unknown — treat the premise as unconfirmed]**. In practice the Garmin path is third-party: Surfr on ~126 models **[stated]**.

**Ground truth used:** only the *Sensors* paper used a real reference (videogrammetry). No vendor publishes a validation protocol **[stated absence]**.

## 2. Academic literature

**The three methods.** The canonical decomposition is numerical double integration (NDI), takeoff velocity (TOV), and flight time (FT) ([Sensors 24(24):7877](https://www.mdpi.com/1424-8220/24/24/7877) / [PMC11679435](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11679435/)) **[stated]**. In that low-sampling-IMU CMJ study NDI won: r = 0.87, bias 2.5 cm, reliability 0.88 **[stated]**. A separate IMU validation found the opposite ordering — flight-time-derived heights ICC 0.93–0.96 vs velocity-derived 0.72–0.78 ([ScienceDirect S2214687318300189](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2214687318300189)) **[stated]**. Note what both assume: **TOV and FT are only valid when gravity is the sole force in flight** — stated explicitly for TOV ("treating the body as a particle under constant acceleration g") **[stated]**. That assumption is exactly what your wing session broke **[inference]**.

**Airtime detection in aerial sports.** Ski/snowboard big-air: boot-mounted IMUs, 833 Hz sampled and transmitted at 54 Hz, smoothing over 185–463 ms windows, jumps = zero-valued regions of vertical acceleration; validated against 25 Hz GoPro video at 150 ms tolerance. Detection rates: 100% for Big Air (n=15), 94% for flight ≥500 ms (n=34), **44% for flights <500 ms** ([PLOS One 10.1371/journal.pone.0307255](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0307255) / [PMC11257225](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11257225/)) **[stated]**. Notably they used **no free-fall threshold** — they smoothed and looked for a zero region **[stated]**, which is a materially different gate from your |a| < 0.35 g **[inference]**.

Chardonnens et al. 2013 instrumented the full ski-jump sequence with seven IMUs, validated against Optotrak; angle errors <6° for 75% of jumps ([J Sports Sci 32(6)](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/02640414.2013.845679)) **[stated]** — relevant as an orientation-estimation precedent, not a height method **[inference]**.

Snowboard halfpipe airtime: a U-Net deep-learning detector outperformed threshold algorithms on IMU ([PMC11548732](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11548732/)) **[stated]** — direct evidence that fixed thresholds are the weak link **[inference]**.

**Lift-during-flight corrections.** I found **none**. No paper I located provides an airtime→height correction for aerodynamically supported flight (kite, wing, ski jump, wingsuit). The physics is described only qualitatively in community sources: the kite keeps generating lift through the jump, decaying as apparent wind falls toward the apex, so the rider is "not a purely ballistic object" ([kiteforum physics thread](https://kiteforum.com/viewtopic.php?t=2307474); [surfertoday](https://www.surfertoday.com/kiteboarding/the-physics-of-kiteboarding)) **[stated, non-peer-reviewed]**. A 2025 biomechanics paper does analyse kite-jump landing loads and argues kite *position* matters more than height for landing impact ([Orthop J Sports Med, doi 10.1177/23259671251369017](https://doi.org/10.1177/23259671251369017)) **[stated]** — evidence the aerodynamic state during flight is measurable and consequential **[inference]**.

**Barometers for 1–10 m jumps.** The best precedent is head-mounted MEMS IMU + barometric pressure for ski/snowboard jump detection: adding vertical velocity and air time to raw acceleration raised sensitivity/specificity to 92%/93% ([J Sports Eng Tech, SAGE 10.1177/1754337115594501](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/1754337115594501)) **[stated]**. That paper uses baro to *detect and classify*, and I did not confirm it uses baro for absolute height **[unknown]**. I found **no** study establishing baro accuracy for 1–10 m amplitudes over 1–4 s **[unknown]** — the sports-watch evidence in watersports is actively negative (above).

## 4. Sensor fusion at 26–50 Hz

What the sources support: WOO-class devices reportedly run Kalman/Madgwick/Mahony orientation then integrate the gravity-removed vertical axis ([kiteforum](https://kiteforum.com/viewtopic.php?t=2418332)) **[stated, unverified]**. The *Sensors* validation shows where that lands in practice: systematic overestimate growing with flight duration, 0.7–0.95 m RMS, because "total error gradually increases with the number of measurements" **[stated]**. **[Inference]** That is the signature of un-corrected integration drift over 3–4 s, and it is the error budget you inherit if you copy the approach.

Structural mitigations the literature supports: ZUPT-style anchoring at takeoff and landing is implied by every flight-time method (both ends are known instants) **[inference]**; the halfpipe U-Net result says learned detectors beat thresholds for the *detection* half **[stated]**. Surfr's 100 Hz floor **[stated]** and its 10 s on-watch airtime cap **[stated]** bound what a real product considers acceptable.

**Honest read on your rate:** at 26–50 Hz, 3.3 s of flight is 85–165 samples. Double integration of a ~0.5 g residual over 3.3 s is metres of error if the bias estimate is off by even 0.01 g **[inference]**. No source I found validates double integration at <100 Hz for multi-second flights.

## 5. What this means for us

The session data is not a detector bug — it is a **model** failure, and the literature confirms nobody has solved the lift-corrected case. Your measured 0.4–0.6 g during wing-supported flight is itself the most valuable signal in the dataset **[inference]**: it says the rider is in flight *and* how much lift is acting, which is exactly the quantity the ballistic model omits. h = g·t²/8 assumes a = −g throughout; with an average net downward acceleration of ~0.5 g, the correct amplitude is roughly a_net·t²/8, and 3.3 s at 0.5 g net gives ~1.6 m ≈ 5.3 ft rather than 13 m **[inference — arithmetic, not a cited result]**. That direction matches the 9.1 ft report far better than 13 m does, though it undershoots, which is expected since lift is not constant through the arc **[inference]**.

### Ranked recommendations

1. **Replace the ballistic formula with a measured-acceleration integration over the detected flight window, not a closed-form t² law.** Integrate the actual vertical specific force (gravity removed, orientation-corrected) twice between takeoff and landing, with a zero-velocity/zero-displacement constraint applied at *both* ends. The both-ends constraint is the single biggest drift killer and it is free — you know both instants. This is the only method in the literature (NDI, bias 2.5 cm vs force plate, [PMC11679435](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11679435/)) that does not assume gravity-only flight.

2. **Replace the |a| < 0.35 g gate.** It is provably missing two-thirds of jumps in wing conditions. Move to the ski big-air pattern — smooth |a| over a ~200–450 ms window and find sustained *low* regions relative to the rider's own riding baseline, not an absolute 0.35 g ([PLOS One](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0307255)). Pair it with the landing spike, which is unambiguous. Expect to keep missing short flights (<500 ms) — even the published algorithm hits only 44% there.

3. **Raise the sample rate before adding any hardware.** Surfr sets a 100 Hz floor and explicitly refuses 50 Hz devices ([Surfr support](https://support.thesurfr.app/en/articles/9859609-which-devices-are-compatible-with-the-surfr-app)). The LSM6DS3 does 104/208/416 Hz natively; going from 26–50 Hz to ≥104 Hz costs a register write and some buffer, and it is a precondition for recommendation 1 being meaningful. Do this *before* concluding the integration approach fails.

4. **Treat the barometer as second, not first.** A BMP390/LPS22 is attractive, but the only watersports evidence in hand is negative (Garmin baro altitude "practically random" on water, immersion-degraded, [Garmin forums](https://forums.garmin.com/outdoor-recreation/outdoor-recreation/f/epix-2/304527/altitude-errors-on-water-sport-activities)), and I found **no** validation of baro for 1–10 m over 1–4 s. Its documented sports use is *detection and classification* alongside an IMU ([SAGE 2015](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/1754337115594501)), which is where it would earn its place: as a drift anchor and an independent airborne flag, not as the primary height source. A sealed, vented-through-a-PTFE-membrane mount is non-trivial on a wet board — cost it honestly.

5. **Build a video ground truth this season, before tuning anything.** Every number in section 1 that means something came from videogrammetry; every number that came from a vendor is unfalsifiable. You don't need four Nikons — a single fixed camera with a known-height reference in frame, time-synced to the puck by a deliberate tap at session start, will resolve 0.3 m at these amplitudes **[inference]**. Without it you will be tuning against Surfr, which the one real study shows overestimates 75% of jumps by up to 1.42 m.

**Open questions I could not close:** whether WOO contains a barometer; whether Garmin ships any native kite/wing jump metric; whether any published work gives a lift-corrected airtime→height relation (I believe none exists, which makes it a genuine contribution if you derive one from your own 0.4–0.6 g traces).
